Urban analytics / Recorded enforcement · Penn MUSA 5500 · Academic project

Parking violations, made visible.

Exploring Philadelphia’s 2017 parking tickets

My role
Data preparation, temporal exploration, and chart design
Tools
Python · Pandas · Matplotlib · Seaborn · Altair
Recorded outcome
Combined half-year records and visual comparisons

The question

When and where are parking tickets recorded, and how do their categories vary? This project explores Philadelphia’s 2017 parking records as a way to practice translating a large administrative dataset into readable visual comparisons.

My contribution

I combined the two half-year data files, prepared temporal and spatial fields, and used Matplotlib, Seaborn, and interactive Altair charts to investigate the records. The notebook preserves the chart code and data-preparation steps.

Reading the evidence

Time and category comparisons help identify patterns in the recorded tickets. They reflect both parking behavior and the process through which violations are observed and recorded. Counts alone do not reveal the share of drivers violating rules or the intensity of enforcement relative to traffic.

What comes next

A stronger comparison would account for street use, available parking, traffic volumes, and enforcement exposure. The project is an exploratory visualization of administrative records, with the source notebook available for inspection.
