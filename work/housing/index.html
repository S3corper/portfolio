Urban analytics / Spatial equity · Penn MUSA 5500 · Academic project

Where housing pressures overlap.

Mapping evictions and code violations in Philadelphia

My role
Data preparation, spatial joins, interactive mapping and interpretation
Tools
Python · GeoPandas · hvPlot · Pandas
Recorded outcome
34,108 selected violation records mapped

The question

Where do eviction filings and housing-code violations concentrate in Philadelphia, and how much do their spatial patterns overlap? This analysis connects administrative records to census geography to make a neighborhood-scale comparison.

My contribution

I prepared tract-level eviction records, cleaned violation coordinates, reprojected the layers, and joined violation points to tract polygons. I created interactive choropleths with selectors for year and violation type, then compared 2016 evictions with selected 2012–2016 violation records.

The workflow

The eviction panel contains 5,376 tract-year rows: 384 Philadelphia tracts across 14 years. The supplied violations file contains 434,052 records. Selecting 15 violation types yielded 34,108 records, all spatially matched to a tract in the saved run.

What the analysis showed

Some high-count areas overlap in North and West Philadelphia. Among the 20 most frequent violation types, the three smallest absolute correlations with 2016 eviction counts were VIOL C&I MESSAGE (r = −0.127), LICENSE-RES GENERAL (r = 0.157), and EXT A-VACANT LOT CLEAN/MAINTAI (r = 0.209). These are relative comparisons of tract counts.

What the result means

The maps compare different time windows and do not connect individual complaints to later eviction cases. Counts are not normalized by rental housing or population. Geographic overlap therefore does not demonstrate retaliatory eviction or a causal relationship. The notebook also contains a separate Landsat NDVI exercise, sampling 2,480 street-tree points.
