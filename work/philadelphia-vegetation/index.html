Remote sensing / Urban vegetation · Penn MUSA 5500 · Academic project

Seeing the city’s vegetation.

A Landsat view of Philadelphia’s vegetation

My role
Raster interpretation, geographic comparison, and tree-point sampling
Tools
Python · GeoPandas · Landsat · NDVI
Recorded outcome
Median NDVI: 0.202 city · 0.375 surrounding rectangle

The question

How does the vegetation signal inside Philadelphia compare with the surrounding area in the same Landsat scene? This exercise complements the housing analysis with a separate view of urban land cover.

The workflow

I compared NDVI inside the city boundary with the surrounding portion of its bounding rectangle and sampled the raster at 2,480 street-tree points. NDVI contrasts near-infrared and red reflectance to describe a vegetation-related spectral signal.

What the analysis showed

The recorded median NDVI was 0.202 inside the city and 0.375 outside the city but within the comparison rectangle. The difference describes those two spatial subsets in the supplied imagery, rather than a population-weighted measure of neighborhood access to greenery.

Scale matters

At 30 m resolution, a Landsat pixel can mix trees, pavement, buildings, and other surfaces. A raster value at a street-tree coordinate is therefore not a direct measurement of that individual tree’s health. The shared notebook also contains the separate housing and eviction analysis.

Coursework adapted from supplied course materials. Results are taken from the saved notebook; the analysis was not rerun for this portfolio.
