Housing markets / Data visualization · Penn MUSA 5500 · Academic project

The Philadelphia donut effect.

Comparing home-value growth inside and outside Center City

My role
Data reshaping, ZIP-code grouping, growth calculation, and visual interpretation
Tools
Python · Pandas · Zillow ZHVI
Recorded outcome
6.08% Center City · 19.32% other Philadelphia ZIP codes

The question

Did home values grow more quickly outside Philadelphia’s central area during the first two years of the pandemic? The donut-effect exercise compares Zillow Home Value Index (ZHVI) appreciation for ZIP codes in greater Center City with other ZIP codes in the notebook’s Philadelphia subset.

The workflow

I reshaped the monthly ZHVI table into tidy records, separated Center City ZIP codes from the remaining Philadelphia ZIP codes, and calculated 100 × (March 2022 value / March 2020 value − 1) for each ZIP code. I then averaged those percentage changes within each group.

Defining the center

The notebook’s greater Center City list is 19123, 19102, 19103, 19106, 19107, 19109, 19130, 19146, and 19147. The comparison group consists of the remaining ZIP codes in the Philadelphia data subset. It is not a sample of the entire metropolitan region or all suburbs.

What the chart shows

The saved means are 6.075356% for Center City and 19.315452% for the comparison group. Rounded to two decimals, that is 6.08% and 19.32%, a difference of 13.24 percentage points. Both groups appreciated, with substantially faster average growth outside the selected central ZIP codes.

What the result means

This pattern is consistent with the descriptive donut-effect idea. The group means are unweighted across ZIP codes, not weighted by housing stock, population, or transactions. This comparison does not isolate remote work, migration, housing supply, or other causes. The visualization reproduces saved notebook results rather than a new market-data download.
