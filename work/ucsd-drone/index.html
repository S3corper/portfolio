The project

A campus map becomes more useful when people can see the buildings, terrain, and spaces between them in three dimensions. Working with the UC San Diego team, I helped capture around 52,000 drone images across 22+ flight sessions to support a campus-scale 3D model.

My contribution

As a Drone Flight Observer / Pilot & GCP Collector from March 2025 to July 2026, I operated and assisted with campus drone surveys and collected and logged ground control points. I processed aerial imagery with ArcGIS SiteScan, ArcGIS Reality Studio, and ArcGIS Pro to create orthomosaics and 3D mapping products, including meshes and Gaussian splats.

From fieldwork to a scene

The workflow connects flight operations, ground reference measurements, imagery processing, and web delivery. Ground control points provide spatial reference for the reconstruction. The resulting products let users move from an overhead image to a view of building form and terrain. No numerical positional-accuracy assessment is reported here.

Explore the campus

The full beta scene includes saved viewpoints for places such as Geisel Library, Triton Center, and the colleges. Users can navigate the model, change the basemap, measure distances or areas, and explore daylight, weather, and shadow settings. These tools help communicate spatial relationships and viewing conditions; modeled shadows are simulations.

A collaborative model

The viewer credits imagery from the 2024–2025 academic year and collaboration with the Cultural Heritage Engineering Initiative (CHEI) Lab, including Scripps Institution of Oceanography drone operations and Geisel Library imagery. My contribution sits within that wider team effort. The beta viewer is the place to explore the complete model.

The imagery and flight totals describe the team effort. Role and workflow are documented in my GIS résumé; the campus viewer remains in beta.
